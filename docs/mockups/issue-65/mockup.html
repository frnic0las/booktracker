Anatomy — 4× zoom, over a dark and a light cover

The scrim is the whole design. Cover art is arbitrary — dark, light, busy, blank — so the badge brings its own background and never depends on what it lands on.

Geometry

Sizeh-4 min-w-6 px-1.5 — 16px tall, 24px min-width, 6px horizontal padding
PositionCover top-right, -top-1 -right-1 (4px) — overhangs the corner, so it never covers cover art the user is reading
Radius6px (rounded-md) — the existing cover radius. No new radius.
TypeSans 9px / 700 / tracking-wider, uppercase. Not mono: font-mono maps to Geist Mono, which has no advantage at two glyphs.
Scrimbg-black/80 + backdrop-blur-sm
Borderborder-white/20 — separates the chip from a dark cover
Texttext-white — 12.6:1 in the worst case (pure-white cover), higher on every darker one
Hit testingpointer-events-none; the row keeps its full 86px target
A11yaria-hidden; the group header announces the source once per group

The badge sits on cover art, not on the page canvas — and cover art is just as likely to be light in dark mode as in light mode. So it stays white-on-dark in both schemes, like the camera overlays in issue #59. Nothing to invert, nothing to test twice.

Rejected — hue-coded badges

Dune
OL
Dune
GB
Reading

The obvious first pass — amber --reading for OL, blue --accent for GB — fails twice. Amber is already the “Reading” status dot (right, from AddBookSheet) — the sheet you land on by tapping one of these very rows. And blue already means “tappable” everywhere in the app, including the “Add to library” button under the badge in board 5. Every other hue in the palette is spoken for too: --read green, --want indigo, --destructive red. There is no free hue — so the badge takes none, and the letters do the work.
